## Overview

Life Goes On is a short, playable game that lets people move through grief as a journey. It isn't about forgetting someone. It's about learning how to carry the loss differently.

The player enters a series of quiet rooms. Each one is dim and still until they solve a small puzzle and act on it — then the light returns. I designed, illustrated and built the whole game myself.

</Split>

Grief is one of the most universal human experiences, yet games almost never make room for it.

## Key design decisions

### 1. Light is the feedback

The core idea: **the room heals with the player.** Every scene opens in shadow. When the player acts, the light warms, colour returns and the space opens up. There are no scores or progress bars — the room itself shows that something has changed, so players *feel* progress instead of reading it.

### 2. Puzzles are pauses, not tests

The puzzles are deliberately easy. Their job is to slow the player down and invite them to look closely — not to frustrate them. There are no timers, no lives and no way to fail. Each answer also names the action that follows: *regrow*, *candle*.

### 3. Real objects make it personal

Grief is always about someone specific, so the rooms are full of real things: a handwritten letter, children's drawings, and my own painting of a whirling dervish on the table beside the candle. These details made the game feel lived in rather than generic.

### 4. A calm visual language

Soft sage walls, warm clay and wood, and gold for anything hopeful — a candle flame, a key, a string of lights. One serif typeface, gentle textures and slow transitions keep the whole experience quiet, so nothing pulls the player out of the moment.

## Building the prototype

I built the game entirely in Canva. Every state of every room is its own screen, and links on buttons and objects move the player forward — 31 screens in all. Working this way let me iterate on the art and the flow at the same time, and share a playable game with nothing more than a link.

## The ending

After the last room, the player arrives somewhere new: a bright, calm, lived-in room. Life goes on — not because the loss is gone, but because there's room to hold it. The game closes with an invitation for players to share their own thoughts.
