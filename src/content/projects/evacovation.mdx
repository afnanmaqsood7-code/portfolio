## Overview

In an emergency, buildings still rely on alarms, radios and people shouting instructions. Evacovation puts the whole response in one app. The chief warden declares what is happening, floor wardens get clear instructions, staff confirm where they are, and everyone can see progress until the building is safe.

I designed the app from scratch to final: the role flows, information architecture, wireframes, every high fidelity screen, a clickable prototype in Adobe XD, and the campaign for its launch.

## The challenge

People use this app at the worst possible moment: stressed, rushed and sometimes in smoke or noise. Every screen had to be understood in a glance and every action had to be hard to get wrong.

## Visual design

In an emergency, colour has to mean the same thing every time. Red is danger or stop, yellow is wait or attention, green is safe or go. Charcoal panels hold live status so it reads even in bright sunlight or smoke.

## Stakeholders and constraints

Safety apps are judged in the worst moment, so the client's priority was speed and zero ambiguity. I aligned the flows with how their wardens already ran drills, then removed every step that did not help someone get out safely.

- One emergency flow shared by every emergency type, so wardens learn it once
- Confirmation only on actions that affect other people
- A strict colour code agreed with the client and used on every screen
