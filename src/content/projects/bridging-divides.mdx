## Overview

The Forum is the internal reporting system for Bridging Divides, a research program at Toronto Metropolitan University. More than 60 researchers and 10 staff use it to record their work, and it feeds the reports the program sends to its funders.

I redesigned its report template so every kind of research output is easy to record, track and report, including the work that never fit the old form.

## The problem

The old template was built for one kind of output: papers and conferences. Everything else fell through the cracks.

  items={[

## My role

UX designer and strategist for four months, working with one developer and the ten staff who manage outputs for 60+ researchers. I led the audit, the information architecture, the sketches and wireframes, and the documentation for the developer.

## Research

I started with the real reports, not assumptions.

<Feature src={audit} alt="Two annotated research reports with outputs highlighted and labelled as publication, activity or conference." kind="screen" kicker="Audit" title="Reading every report line by line">

I went through the program's reports and labelled each output by type. This showed how much work was hidden inside long paragraphs, and how often it was filed in the wrong category.

</Feature>

<Feature reverse src={sidebar} alt="The Forum sidebar with People, Manage Outputs, Collaborations and Review Duplicates." kind="screen" kicker="Gap analysis" title="Matching the Forum to the funder">

I compared the Forum's categories with the funder's reporting categories and marked every output that was missing or in the wrong place. Short sessions with staff then showed how they actually track outputs day to day.

</Feature>

Publications, Activities, Awards, Research Tools and a cross cutting set of equity tags. Each one has example outputs and a clear purpose, so people know where their work belongs.

</Feature>

<Feature reverse src={template} alt="Category list for publications, activities, awards, digital technologies and institutional outputs." kind="screen" kicker="Taxonomy" title="A place for every output">

Inside each category I listed the real output types I found in the audit, from policy briefs and media mentions to data portals and sharing circles.

</Feature>

<Feature src={fields} alt="Field specification table: output title, type, authors, date, link and citation, with guidance for each." kind="screen" kicker="Form" title="Fields with guidance built in">

Every field explains what to enter, with an example. Citations fill in from a DOI, and the citation field only appears for publications, so people see less and enter less.

</Feature>

## Key decisions

<Points
  items={[
    { title: 'Add to it, don\'t replace it', body: 'We kept the existing Manage Outputs section and built on it, so staff could switch without disruption.' },
    { title: 'Design for staff first', body: 'Researchers rarely log their own work, so the template follows the staff workflow, with bulk entry and auto fill.' },
    { title: 'A home for research tools', body: 'A new Research Tools and Infrastructure module for dashboards, data portals and protocols.' },
    { title: 'Numbers and stories together', body: 'Participant counts, equity tags and progress status sit next to traditional outputs.' },
  ]}

## Results

Reporting went from manual and inconsistent to structured and fast. Staff estimate it is about 60% faster, reports need fewer corrections, and the program can finally show the community, equity and technical work that used to be invisible.

<Quote text="The template now captures the full breadth of research impact, not just the papers." accent="not just the papers." />

## Reflection

<Points
  items={[
    { title: 'Design for the real users', body: 'The people who report are not always the people who do the work. Finding that out changed the whole design.' },
