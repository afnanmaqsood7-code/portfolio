## Overview

The Forum is the internal reporting system for Bridging Divides, a research program at Toronto Metropolitan University. More than 60 researchers and 10 staff use it to record their work, and it feeds the reports the program sends to its funders.

I redesigned its report template so every kind of research output is easy to record, track and report, including the work that never fit the old form.

## The problem

The old template was built for one kind of output: papers and conferences. Everything else fell through the cracks.

## Research

I started with the real reports, not assumptions.

<Feature src={audit} alt="Two annotated research reports with outputs highlighted and labelled as publication, activity or conference." kind="screen" kicker="Audit" title="Reading every report line by line">

I went through the program's reports and labelled each output by type. This showed how much work was hidden inside long paragraphs, and how often it was filed in the wrong category.

</Feature>

<Feature src={forumHome} alt="The Forum: a dark sidebar with People and Outputs sections beside the welcome page." kind="screen" kicker="Gap analysis" title="Matching the Forum to the funder">

I compared the Forum's categories with the funder's reporting categories and marked every output that was missing or in the wrong place. Short sessions with staff then showed how they actually track outputs day to day.

</Feature>

### Five clear categories

Every output now has an obvious home, with examples and a clear purpose.

| Category | Example outputs | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Publications | Papers, policy briefs, infographics, media mentions | Scholarly and public work |
| Activities | Pilot projects, VR experiments, workshops, focus groups | Community and training work |
| Awards | Stipends, travel funds, faculty and trainee awards | Recognition and funding |
| Research tools | Dashboards, data portals, software, protocols | Technical contributions |
| Equity tags | Applied across every category | Who the research reaches |

### Fields with guidance built in

Each field tells people what to enter, with an example, so entries come in clean the first time.

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Output title | A clear name, like "Digital Dashboard for Settlement Agencies" |
| Output type | Chosen from a list for the selected category |
| Authors | Full names of everyone who contributed |
| Date | Month and year, like "March 2024" |
| Link or file | Where the output lives, such as a website or Drive link |
| Citation | Filled in automatically from the DOI, shown only for publications |

## Key decisions

<Points
  items={[
    { title: 'Add to it, don\'t replace it', body: 'We built on the existing Manage Outputs section, so staff could switch without disruption.' },
    { title: 'Design for staff first', body: 'Researchers rarely log their own work, so the template follows the staff workflow, with bulk entry and auto fill.' },
    { title: 'Show work in progress', body: 'An in progress status makes ongoing projects visible before they are published.' },
    { title: 'Numbers and stories together', body: 'Participant counts and equity tags sit next to traditional outputs, so impact is described, not just counted.' },
  ]}
/>

## Results

Reporting went from manual and inconsistent to structured and fast. Staff estimate it is about 60% faster, reports need fewer corrections, and the program can finally show the community, equity and technical work that used to be invisible.
