## Overview

Glitchard Bugson is an AI powered MetaHuman comedian, "arrested for public lagging". He talks with an audience in real time, improvises and tells jokes in his own voice.

I made him from scratch over nine months. I developed his character and comedy style, built his body as a MetaHuman in Unreal Engine, gave him a mind and a voice in Convai, connected the two, and trained him session after session until his jokes landed.

## The question

Can an AI character be genuinely funny, or does it just repeat jokes?

<Feature src={question} alt="Slide with the research question about designing AI powered MetaHumans with a comedic personality." kind="screen" kicker="Research question" title="Designing a personality, not a joke machine">

How can AI powered MetaHumans be designed to embody a comedic personality and deliver engaging comedic performances? A chatbot can tell a joke. A comedian needs character, timing and the ability to read a room.

</Feature>

## Research

<Feature src={bergson} alt="Bergson's humour theory slide: things are funny when something alive acts a little bit like a robot." kind="screen" kicker="Theory" title="Funny when the alive acts like a robot">

Henri Bergson argued that we laugh when something alive behaves a little like a machine. An AI comedian flips this: a machine that acts almost human. That tension became the heart of Glitchard's humour. He glitches, lags and knows it.

</Feature>

## How I worked

<Feature src={approach} alt="Approach diagram: research question, practice based research over nine months, Glitchard." kind="screen" kicker="Approach" title="Learning by building">

This was practice based research: Glitchard is the research. Over nine months I built, tested with people and changed him, again and again. Every version taught me something about what makes an AI character feel alive.

</Feature>

<Feature src={tech} alt="Two technical workflows compared, with the Convai, ElevenLabs and Unreal Engine pipeline." kind="screen" kicker="Tech" title="Choosing the stack">

I compared two workflows and chose Convai for conversation and character memory, ElevenLabs for his voice and Unreal Engine 5 with MetaHuman for his body and face, all running live.

</Feature>

<Feature src={concept} alt="Character development notes with questions about comedy style, quirks and emotional state, beside an emotion wheel." kind="screen" kicker="01 Character" title="Who is Glitchard?">

I started with three questions. What is his comedy style? What is his emotional state? What are his quirks? I wrote his backstory and used an emotion wheel to map how he reacts, so he feels like a person with moods, not a script.

</Feature>

<Feature src={appearance} alt="Glitchard's face, full body MetaHuman model and stage render." kind="screen" kicker="02 Appearance" title="A face made for comedy">

I designed his look as a MetaHuman: big expressive eyes, a tiny moustache and a casual outfit. He had to read as funny at first glance and still look natural when he moves and talks.

</Feature>

<Feature src={convai} alt="Convai dashboard with Glitchard's character settings, personality sliders and knowledge bank." kind="screen" kicker="03 Mind" title="Bringing him to life in Convai">

In Convai I set his personality traits, speaking style and knowledge bank, so his answers stay in character whatever the audience says.

</Feature>

<Feature src={unreal} alt="Unreal Engine blueprints connected to Convai, with Glitchard rendered on screen." kind="screen" kicker="04 Integration" title="Connecting mind and body">

I connected Convai to Unreal Engine, so his voice, lip sync, facial expressions and gestures all respond live to the conversation.

</Feature>

<Feature src={training} alt="Training session logs with Glitchard's replies and notes, beside Glitchard talking." kind="screen" kicker="05 Training" title="Training him to be funny">

I ran session after session, scoring each joke, noting where timing failed or catchphrases repeated, then adjusting his settings and knowledge. This loop is what turned scripted lines into real comedy.

</Feature>
